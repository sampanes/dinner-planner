# Cooking with kids

## How to use this guide

This guide sorts the site's 70 recipes three ways: dinners a picky eater will usually accept as written, dinners that are easy to split into a plain plate and a full-flavor plate, and dinners that work well for teaching a child to cook. Pick a recipe from the list that fits tonight, or look any recipe up in the full table at the end. The verdicts come from reading each recipe's ingredients and steps. They are judgments, not guarantees, and every child is different. The general advice and the age bands come from the pediatric, dietetic, and extension sources listed at the bottom.

## House rules

What has worked at this table, and it overrides the verdicts below where they disagree:

- **Any pasta works.** Lift the child's pasta out at the drain step, before it meets the sauce, and put the sauce on the side. Plain meat comes out too wherever it is browned before the sauce goes in. The exceptions are the four dishes where the pasta cooks in the sauce (One Pot Spicy Rigatoni alla Rossa, Italian Sausage Rigatoni, and the two one-pan tortelloni bakes). For those, boil a small pot of the same pasta on the side.
- **Burgers work the same way.** Build the child's burger plain and leave the toppings off.
- **Skip beef and beans.** [Beef & Black Bean Chili](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/beef-black-bean-chili.html) and [Beef Chili Bar](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/beef-chili-bar.html) do not go over here, adults included. Two other recipes are built on beans too: [Pork Chili 'n' Cheese Stuffed Poblanos](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/pork-chili-n-cheese-stuffed-poblanos.html) and [Cheesy Black Bean Tortilla Crunch Skillet](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/cheesy-black-bean-tortilla-crunch-skillet.html).

## General principles

**Feeding a picky eater**

- **Split the jobs.** The adult decides what food is offered, when, and where. The child decides whether to eat it and how much. Pressure tends to backfire. (Ellyn Satter Institute)
- **Put one familiar food on the table at every meal,** and do not cook a separate dinner when a child refuses. Let the child choose from what is offered. (HealthyChildren.org)
- **Serve the parts separately.** A deconstructed meal keeps the ingredients apart instead of mixed, and each person builds a plate. Tacos, bowls, quesadillas, and pasta all work this way. Introduce one new food at a time, next to familiar ones. (Children's Health)
- **Keep offering, without pushing.** Sources give different counts, and all of them are high: 8 to 10 exposures (CDC), 12 to 15 (Children's Health), 15 to 20 tries (HealthyChildren.org). Offer a small portion, accept a "no," and wait about a week before trying again.
- **Foods that must not touch are normal.** Favoring a few foods and wanting them kept apart often fades by about age 5. (CDC)
- **Cook together.** Children who help choose and prepare food are more likely to try it eventually. (HealthyChildren.org)

**Kitchen jobs by age**

Ages are rough. Skill and experience matter more than the birthday, and every band assumes an adult is in the room.

| Age | Jobs that usually fit |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3 | Wash produce, snap green beans, tear herbs and lettuce, squeeze citrus, sprinkle, stir |
| 3 to 5 | Mix cold ingredients, brush on oil, mash potatoes or beans, spread, pour, roll and shape dough |
| 5 to 7 | Measure dry ingredients, juice fruit, snip herbs and scallions with clean blunt kitchen shears, grate cheese, zest, form patties, grease a pan, rinse beans |
| 7 to 9 | Use a can opener and a peeler, pound chicken, check meat with a food thermometer, use a pizza cutter |
| 9 to 12, with supervision | Boil pasta, use the microwave, simmer on the stove, bake in the oven, slice and chop vegetables, follow a recipe from start to finish |

Sources: Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Illinois Extension, The Kids Cook Monday. They do not agree exactly. Illinois Extension lists cutting with a small knife at 5 to 7 depending on the child's skill, while the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics places slicing and chopping at 9 to 12 with supervision. This guide takes the cautious reading: the knife stays with the adult until a child has been taught and is being watched.

**Safety basics**

- **Hands.** Wash with soap for at least 20 seconds before cooking and again after touching raw meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, or flour. (CDC)
- **Raw meat stays separate.** Use one cutting board and plate for raw meat, poultry, and seafood and another for food that will not be cooked. (CDC)
- **No tasting until it is cooked.** No licking fingers, and a tasting spoon does not go back in the pot. Tie back long hair. (Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics)
- **Cook to temperature, checked with a thermometer:** ground beef and pork 160 F, all poultry including ground turkey and chicken 165 F, fish 145 F. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. (CDC)
- **Heat.** Use oven mitts, never leave the stove unattended, and keep pan handles turned in. (Illinois Extension, The Kids Cook Monday)
- **Knives.** When a child is ready, teach the "bear claw" grip for the hand holding the food and use a shorter knife. (America's Test Kitchen)
- **Children ask an adult before they start cooking.** (Nebraska Extension)

In the lists below, "adult" jobs are the ones the sources place at 9 to 12 with supervision or later: knife work, hot pans and hot oil, the oven and broiler, and draining boiling water. Raw meat is an adult job for young children. From about 6 to 7 a child can shape meatballs or patties and then wash hands right away.

## 1. Already picky-eater friendly as written

Eight recipes. Each is mild, has a familiar shape and texture, and has little or nothing to pick out. Under the house rules, every other pasta dinner on the site belongs here too, with the sauce on the side.

- [Cheesy Baked Pasta with Meat Sauce](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/cheesy-baked-pasta-with-meat-sauce.html): penne, ground beef, marinara, and melted mozzarella. The garlic is grated in. No visible vegetables and no heat.
- [Cavatappi Beef Ragu](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/cavatappi-beef-ragu.html): a mild marinara and cream cheese meat sauce on corkscrew pasta. The carrot is chopped fine and cooks into the sauce. The parsley is the only green and can stay off a child's bowl.
- [Rigatoni with Beef & Zucchini Ragu](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/rigatoni-with-beef-zucchini-ragu.html): creamy tomato beef sauce with no chili. The zucchini is grated and squeezed dry, so it disappears into the sauce.
- [Penne with Beef & Zucchini Ragu](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/penne-with-beef-zucchini-ragu.html): the same mild sauce with grated zucchini, on penne.
- [Creamy Tomato Penne & Beef Meatballs](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/creamy-tomato-penne-beef-meatballs.html): plain baked meatballs set on top of penne in a mild pink cream sauce. The diced tomato is a topping added at the bowl, so it is easy to skip.
- [White Cheddar Crunch Burgers](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/white-cheddar-crunch-burgers.html): a salt-and-pepper cheeseburger with oven potato wedges. Burgers are built one at a time, so the mustard and onions go only on the buns that want them.
- [Beef Ragu Pasta Bar](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/beef-ragu-pasta-bar.html): served as separate bowls of plain penne, meat sauce, lemon cream sauce, roasted zucchini, broccoli, and Parmesan. Everyone builds a plate. The carrot is grated into the meat sauce.
- [Family-Style Creamy Chicken Pasta Bake](https://sampanes.github.io/dinner-planner/recipes/family-style-creamy-chicken-pasta-bake.html): penne and chicken in a mild cheddar cream sauce. The one caveat is the broccoli. It is in visible bite-size florets that are easy to leave, but it boils with the pasta and bakes in the dish, so it cannot be kept out without changing the method.
